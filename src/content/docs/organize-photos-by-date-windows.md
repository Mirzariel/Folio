Photos from cameras, phones and old drives rarely arrive in a sensible order. Sorting them into
folders by the date they were taken is the simplest structure that still makes sense in ten
years: you can find a trip by when it happened, and new photos have an obvious place to go.

## Use the date the camera recorded

Folder dates and file timestamps change when you copy, edit or download a file, so they are a poor
guide. Folio uses the date the camera recorded inside the photograph. A photograph without a
recorded date is not guessed at: it stays where it is, and the plan tells you why.

## Choose how deep the folders go

- **Year.** One folder per year.
- **Year and month.** A year folder, then a month folder inside it.
- **Year, month and day.** One folder per day, for large libraries or heavy shooting days.

## Choose how the folders are named

- **Nested.** Folders inside folders, such as `2019\08`.
- **Nested, full date.** Each level repeats the date so far, such as `2019\2019_08`.
- **Dashed.** One folder with dashes, such as `2019-08`.
- **Underscored.** One folder with underscores, such as `2019_08`.

## Organize by date with Folio

1. **Add your locations.** Folders and drives both work, and Folio can hold several at once.
2. **Let it read.** The scan only reads and never changes your files.
3. **Open Organize by date.** Pick the depth and the naming style. The preview updates as you choose.
4. **Read the plan.** Folio lists every folder it would create and every file it would move.
5. **Approve.** Nothing on your drives changes until you do.

## What to know

- Photos, RAW files and videos are catalogued together, and RAW files keep their extension.
- Folio does not yet read the capture date from ORF, RW2, CR3 and RAF files, so organizing by
  date leaves those where they are.
- Photographs that are scanned negatives or exports often carry no recorded date. They are left
  alone rather than filed under a wrong one.

Duplicates make a messy library messier, so it helps to
[find and remove duplicate photos](/docs/find-duplicate-photos-windows) first. When the folders
are in order, you can [rename the photos in bulk](/docs/rename-photos-in-bulk-windows) too.
